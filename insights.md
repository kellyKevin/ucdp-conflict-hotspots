# Research-Backed Insights on Conflict and Economics

## 1. The Conflict Trap (Collier et al., 2003)
Conflict is often self-reinforcing. Countries that have experienced one civil war are significantly more likely to experience another. This is due to the destruction of physical and human capital, as well as the persistence of "grievance" and "greed" (the ability of rebel groups to fund themselves).

## 2. Greed vs. Grievance (Collier & Hoeffler, 2004)
Research suggests that economic factors—specifically the opportunity cost of rebellion—are often stronger predictors of conflict than social or political grievances. Low GDP growth and high dependence on primary commodity exports increase conflict risk.

## 3. Economic Downturns as a Conflict Driver
Studies (e.g., Miguel et al., 2004) have used rainfall shocks as an instrument for economic growth to show that negative growth shocks significantly increase the likelihood of civil conflict in Africa.

## 4. Supply Chain Resilience
Companies operating in high-risk zones must balance the cost of disruption (e.g., blocked shipping routes in the Red Sea) against the cost of rerouting. AI-driven forecasting can reduce the "fog of war" for corporate decision-makers.
